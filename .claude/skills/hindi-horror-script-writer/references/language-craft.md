# Language Craft

## Register and honorifics

Hindi encodes social hierarchy in the second person, and most of these stories are conflicts about hierarchy. Get this right and the script feels authentic; get it wrong and it feels translated.

| Form | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **आप** | Elders, tantriks, priests, village heads, in-laws | तांत्रिक महाराज, आप क्या कह रहे हैं? |
| **तुम** | Peers, friends, spouses, younger relatives | मेघना, तुम ठीक तो हो? |
| **तू** | Intimates, small children — or contempt from the entity | तू इस दुनिया के योग्य नहीं है। |

The entity switching from **आप** to **तू** mid-scene is a free, instantly-readable escalation.

## Dialogue conventions

**No attribution tags.** Never write "मदन ने कहा". The corpus never does. Character is identified by vocative address inside the line itself:

> "मुखिया जी, अब हमें तांत्रिक बाबा को खबर कर देनी चाहिए।"
> "हां, पुजारी जी।"

This saves 5–8% of word count and lets a single narrator carry a multi-character scene. It also means **every line should name who is being spoken to**, so a listener who is half-watching always knows who is in the room. This matters — the audience consumes this content before sleep and during meals, audio-forward, phone in hand.

**Sentence length by beat.** Long and descriptive in beats 1–2 and 6. Short and clipped — three to six words — in beats 4 and 8.

**Tense.** Past tense for narration by default. Switch to **present tense** during high-tension sequences:

> मेघना चौंकते हुए पीछे मुड़कर देखती है और उसके मुंह से चीख निकल जाती है।

## Stock atmospheric phrases

These are genre signals. Their familiarity is the point — they tell the listener what kind of story this is. Use several per script.

| Phrase | Meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|
| मौत का सन्नाटा | a silence of death | Beat 1, Beat 4 |
| रूह कांप उठती है | the soul trembles | Beat 4, Beat 9B |
| खौफ पैदा कर रखा था | had bred terror | Beat 1 |
| आधी रात का पहर | the dead of night | Beat 1, Beat 8 |
| काफी देर बाद | after a long while | after every silent beat |
| उसी समय / तभी | at that moment / just then | scene pivots |
| इसी सोच के साथ | with this thought | transition into action |
| वहां पहुंचते हुए | on reaching there | after travel sequences |
| सन्नाटा चीर दिया | tore the silence apart | before a scream |
| कदमों की आहट | the sound of approaching footsteps | the entity's arrival |

## Sound cue inventory

Use the Hindi cue names. Target **20–24 cues** per script — roughly one designed audio event every 65 seconds. This is what carries the dread; the animation is deliberately simple and the audio compensates.

| Cue | Meaning | Deployment | Per script |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[SFX] संगीत` | music sting / bed | Scene transitions; 2–3 stacked under the ending | 7–8 |
| `[SFX] हंसी` | laughter | Almost always the **entity's** laugh — the primary supernatural signature | 3 |
| `[SFX] चीखने की आवाज़` | scream | Reserved. Beat 4 and Beat 8 only. Scarcity preserves impact. | 1–2 |
| `[SFX] कराहने की आवाज़` | groan / agony | Death and possession | 1 |
| `[SFX] गहरी सांस लेने की आवाज़` | deep breath | Pre-scare tension | 1 |
| `[SFX] हांफने की आवाज़` | panting | Chase sequences | 1 |
| `[SFX] अचानक ज़ोर से सांस लेने की आवाज़` | sudden gasp | Jump-scare punctuation | 1 |
| `[SFX] रोने की आवाज़` | crying | Grief / emotional resolution | 1 |
| `[SFX] पायल` | anklet bells | The classic chudail approach signal | as needed |
| `[SFX] कुत्तों का भौंकना` | dogs barking | Night atmosphere; entity nearby | as needed |
| `[SFX] झींगुर` | crickets | Atmosphere plates. Cutting them dead is itself a scare. | as needed |

**A cut to silence is a sound cue.** Killing the crickets a beat before the entity appears works better than adding a sting.

## Character archetypes

Reuse these. They exist so the animation team can reuse rigs, and so the audience recognises roles instantly.

| Archetype | Function | Register |
|---|---|---|
| **The Elder** (पिताजी / दादा जी / मुखिया जी) | States The Rule in Beat 2. Is proved right. | Receives आप |
| **The Rationalist** | Dismisses The Rule. Is punished. Present in 27/32 corpus scripts. | Uses तुम, is dismissive |
| **The Protagonist** | Young, 18–25. Curious rather than brave. Often female. | Receives तुम |
| **The Tantrik / Priest** | Delivers the Lore Dump in Beat 6. Names the weakness. | Receives आप, speaks formally |
| **The Entity** | Female more often than not. Has a grievance, not just an appetite. | Switches आप → तू |
| **The Witness** | Sees the climax, reacts, confirms it was real in the cliffhanger. | Any |

**The entity should have a grievance.** In the corpus the antagonist is very often a woman who acquired power, or was wronged and returned. This is what separates a story from a monster attack — and it is why these scripts carry emotional weight at the end rather than just relief.

## Naming

Use plain, regionally-plausible names. The corpus uses: मदन, मेघना, अनुज, इशिता, दमयंती, रोहन, सुगना, मोहित, शीतल, विशाल, बिरजू, दीपू, केशव, जगदीश, रत्ना चाचा.

Villages: बेरमपुर, चंदनपुर, जनकपुरी, विजयनगर. Two syllables plus **-पुर** or **-नगर** is the reliable pattern. Invent the village — never use a real haunted location, because real locations have known endings and cannot be serialised.

## What never appears

- Any call to action in the voice track — not once
- Zombies, vampires, werewolves, Western demons, Ouija boards
- Gore, mutilation, on-screen violence against children
- Real named people or real haunted locations
- Communal or caste framing
- Sexual content — romance appears only as a plot trigger, never as content
- Explicitly sectarian resolution mechanics — keep rituals generic so both Hindu and Muslim audiences stay

## Final self-check before building the PDF

- Word count between 2,700 and 3,000?
- The Rule stated by 1:50, by an elder, with a penalty?
- First supernatural signal before 60 s (or 25 s if Part 2+)?
- 12–16 silent beats, totalling roughly 195 seconds?
- 20–24 sound cues?
- Around 35 question marks?
- Zero occurrences of subscribe / like / share / comment / channel name?
- Screen direction stated in every silent beat?
- Rationalist character present and punished?
- Cliffhanger: shock → witness → 3–5 questions (one brand-new) → unconditional promise?
