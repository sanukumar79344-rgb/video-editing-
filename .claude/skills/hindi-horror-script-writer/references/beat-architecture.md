# Beat Architecture

The nine-beat structure. Every high-performing script in the source corpus follows it. Clock timings assume a 23-minute runtime; scale proportionally for other lengths.

Total spoken word budget: **2,850**.

---

## Beat 1 — COLD OPEN
**0:00–0:40 · 80 words · 3% of runtime**

Establish place, time and a wrongness. Nothing else.

**Must contain:**
- A named location (a village, a station, a specific state) — realism device
- A time marker (a clock time, a month, a season, "आधी रात का पहर")
- Something already wrong

**Must NOT contain:**
- Character introductions or backstory
- A channel logo mention or any CTA
- An explanation of the entity

Open on the story already in motion. If this is Part 2 or later, open mid-scene — ideally with the antagonist gaining an advantage — with no recap at all.

**Target: first supernatural signal before 0:60 in Part 1, before 0:25 in Part 2+.** In the source corpus, breakout episodes landed it at a median of 23 seconds; non-breakout episodes at 191 seconds. This is the single biggest measurable difference between them.

---

## Beat 2 — THE RULE
**0:40–1:50 · 140 words · 3–8% of runtime**

The most important device in this category. An authority figure — father, grandmother, village head, priest — states a prohibition and its penalty.

**Structure:**
1. The prohibition, in the imperative: *"...मत करना"*
2. The condition that triggers it
3. The penalty, stated flatly

**Example from the corpus:**

> ठीक है, लेकिन ध्यान रहे — जैसे ही तुमको उनके कदमों की आहट सुनाई दे और उनका वो मंत्र जिसको वो दोहराते हुए आगे बढ़ते हैं, तो उन्हें बिना देखे किसी ऐसी जगह छिप जाना जहां से ना वो तुमको देख सके, ना तुम उनको। वो चार भयानक आदमी होते हैं जो अपने कंधों पर एक ताबूत उठाए होते हैं — और अगर कोई उन्हें देख ले, तो वो जिंदा नहीं बचता।

**Why it works:** it creates a contract with the viewer inside 20 seconds. The audience now knows the win condition and the fail condition, so every later scene is legible as *"are they about to break the rule?"* It generates dramatic irony for free, it is memorable and quotable, it costs nothing to animate (two lines in a static two-shot), and it mirrors prohibitions that real households actually issue.

**Write The Rule before you write anything else.** Then build the plot as a sequence of near-violations followed by one real violation. This inverts the normal writing order and it is why these scripts feel structurally identical yet never boring.

## Beat 3 — THE VIOLATION
**1:50–3:30 · 200 words · 8–15% of runtime**

The protagonist dismisses The Rule out loud, then acts on it.

**Must contain the rationalist line** — a modern, sceptical dismissal spoken aloud. From the corpus:

> ऐसा लगता है कि हमारे गांव के लोग अभी भी दूसरी सदी में जी रहे हैं।

This character is always wrong and is always punished. Present in 27 of 32 source scripts. It exists so the audience's inherited belief is validated at the end.

End the beat with the protagonist physically crossing a threshold — leaving the house, entering the forest, boarding the train.

---

## Beat 4 — FIRST CONTACT
**3:30–5:45 · 280 words · 15–25% of runtime**

Direct encounter with the entity, but **partial** — a sound, a silhouette, backlit, at frame edge, glimpsed and gone. Never a full reveal here.

**Must contain:** the first `[SFX] चीखने की आवाज़` (scream) of the script. Screams are scarce — 1 to 2 per script — and their scarcity is what preserves their impact.

Follow the scare with a `[SILENT 8–15s]` beat before anyone reacts. The pause is the scare.

---

## Beat 5 — ESCALATION CYCLES
**5:45–12:40 · 860 words · 25–55% of runtime**

The longest stretch. Three or four repeating units of roughly two minutes each:

> **investigate → discover → threaten → survive**

Each cycle must escalate on at least one axis: proximity (further away → inside the house), stakes (a stranger → a family member), or knowledge (a rumour → physical evidence).

**Pacing here is where scripts die.** Keep tension markers at 2–4 per two-minute cycle. Put a `[SILENT]` travel beat between cycles that change location, and an atmosphere plate between cycles that don't.

---

## Beat 6 — THE LORE DUMP
**12:40–16:00 · 415 words · 55–70% of runtime**

A tantrik, priest or village elder explains the entity: its origin, its rules, its weakness. Usually delivered over a flashback.

Present in 29 of 32 source scripts. This is the **mid-video retention anchor** — the point at which a viewer who has come this far commits to finishing, because they have just been promised that the threat is solvable.

**Must contain:**
- An origin (who this was before they became this)
- The rule the entity itself obeys
- A named weakness — an object, a mantra, a ritual, a condition

Put the flashback in a visually different world from the present-day story. If the present is a village at night, make the flashback a daylit palace. The register switch is what stops 23 minutes looking like six backgrounds.

---

## Beat 7 — THE PLAN
**16:00–19:00 · 375 words · 70–82% of runtime**

The counter-measure is assembled. Name the objects and the steps specifically — not "a ritual" but "seven lamps, mustard seeds, and the thread from her wedding sari, at the hour before dawn."

Specificity is what makes the audience believe the mechanism, and belief is what makes the climax land.

End the beat with someone voicing the risk: *"अगर ये काम नहीं आया तो?"*

---

## Beat 8 — CONFRONTATION
**19:00–21:50 · 355 words · 82–95% of runtime**

The ritual is executed. This is the densest sound block in the script — cluster screams, groans, gasps and music here.

Switch narration to **present tense** for this beat. The corpus does this specifically during high-tension sequences:

> मेघना चौंकते हुए पीछे मुड़कर देखती है और उसके मुंह से चीख निकल जाती है।

Keep dialogue short here. Lines of three to six words. Long sentences kill urgency.

---

## Beat 9 — THE ENDING
**21:50–23:00 · 145 words · 95–100% of runtime**

Two versions. Choose based on whether this is a series episode or a standalone.

### 9A — Cliffhanger (use for series episodes)

Four parts, in order. See `references/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md` for full detail.

1. Sensory shock — a scream or a horror image, not an explanation
2. Witness reaction — other characters register it, confirming it was real
3. Question stack — 3 to 5 open questions, escalating in scope, at least one introducing a brand-new unknown
4. The promise — unconditional: *"इन सभी सवालों के जवाब जानने के लिए हमारे साथ जुड़े रहिए, इस कहानी के अगले भाग में।"*

Run 2–3 music cues under the whole sequence and let them continue past the last word, holding the viewer through the end screen.

### 9B — Closed loop (use for standalones and final parts)

Threat destroyed, community restored, then a lingering atmospheric image that leaves a residue of doubt.

> लेकिन आज भी बरसात की पहली रात में, पुराने बरगद के पेड़ के पास खड़े होकर अगर कोई ध्यान से देखे, तो उसे ऐसा महसूस होता है जैसे एक माँ दूर खड़ी अपने बेटे को मुस्कुरा कर देख रही हो।

Cliffhanger episodes averaged 4× the views and 4× the comment rate of closed-loop episodes on the same channel with the same team. Default to the cliffhanger unless the user has asked for a one-off.
